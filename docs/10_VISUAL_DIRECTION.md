# Visual Direction

## Product identity

The visual direction is **Digital Tabletop Arcade**.

The experience should feel like a polished digital adaptation of a social card game rather than a web dashboard.

Core qualities:

- playful;
- colorful;
- tactile;
- readable;
- social;
- casual-premium;
- expressive without becoming noisy.

## Core principles

### The table is the scene
The table should occupy most of the viewport. UI chrome is secondary.

### The player's hand is primary
The local hand must be the most prominent interactive area, especially on mobile.

### The center is the action stage
Reserve the center for deck, discard/action stage, hidden-card indicator, and temporary event emphasis.

### Opponents are peripheral but legible
Opponent seats show avatar, name, victory tokens, protection, elimination, connection state, public discards, and hidden hand count/back where appropriate.

### Game UI, not dashboard UI
Avoid dense SaaS panels, spreadsheet-like layouts, settings sidebars, and excessive glassmorphism.

Prefer cards, badges, chips, compact HUD elements, layered tabletop surfaces, and focused game-state feedback.

## Tone

Use a whimsical, bright, casual-premium style.

Avoid dark cyberpunk, casino realism, medieval fantasy, excessive skeuomorphism, or productivity-app minimalism.

## Color

Color may communicate:

- current turn;
- selection;
- legal target;
- protection;
- elimination;
- winner;
- disabled state;
- private decision focus.

Never rely on color alone. Use text, icons, borders, opacity, or shape too.

## Depth and material

Use subtle physicality:

- soft card shadows;
- visible deck thickness;
- subtle tabletop texture;
- slight elevation on selected cards;
- clear center-stage depth.

Avoid heavy photorealism or exaggerated 3D.

## Typography

Use a highly legible sans-serif for interface text.

Card value/title may use a more expressive display face.

Card values must remain readable at small sizes.

## Visual hierarchy

Primary:
- current action;
- own hand;
- current turn.

Secondary:
- deck/discard;
- legal targets;
- private-decision context.

Tertiary:
- opponent metadata;
- public discard history;
- round/match score.

Decorative:
- particles;
- table accents;
- environment art.

Decorative elements must never compete with gameplay.

## Background environment

The table may sit inside a stylized environment, but the background must remain quieter than the table and tolerate responsive cropping.

## IP boundary

Do not copy UNO logos, card backs, branded table art, or protected Power Hungry Pets artwork unless rights are confirmed.

Use references only for composition and quality.

## Success criteria

A successful screen should answer within a second:

- Is it my turn?
- What can I do?
- What cards do I have?
- Who can I target?
- Who is protected?
- Who is eliminated?
- What just happened?
- Who is winning?
